# Design Benchmark Scorecard

## Purpose

This is the working benchmark for refining the personal website toward 10/10 execution across content, visual design, clarity, and experience.

Use this file to:
- Preserve the current baseline.
- Track which categories need refinement.
- Define section-level content jobs before rewriting.
- Prevent new copy from increasing cognitive load.
- Revisit scores after each major content or design pass.

Overall baseline: 6.4/10

Core diagnosis:

The site has atmosphere, restraint, and a strong authored world, but the baseline was previously overrated. The main visual issue is contrast and depth: the beige paper system is washing out the page, causing surfaces, diagrams, cards, and metadata to blend into the background. The main content issue is clarity: the page still carries too much scaffolding language, repeated metadata, and explanatory structure. Together, the site risks feeling pale, low-depth, and cognitively heavier than the clarity-first positioning demands.

Correction note:

The original review over-rewarded intention. Future scoring should judge perception first: if the visitor experiences the page as washed out, low-contrast, or flat, the score must reflect that regardless of how coherent the underlying design rationale is.

## Refinement Rule

Each section rewrite must answer:

1. What decision should the visitor make after this section?
2. What is the one takeaway?
3. Which words can be removed without losing meaning?
4. Which labels are internal scaffolding rather than visitor-facing clarity?
5. Does the section reduce cognitive load?

If a rewrite makes the page feel more impressive but less clear, reject it.

## Hero Banner Workstream

### Source Job

From the homepage narrative and guardrail artifacts:

- Establish identity, positioning, emotional tone, and intellectual clarity.
- Create authority and curiosity.
- Immediately communicate systems thinking, operational AI, behavioral intelligence, experimentation, and measurable outcomes.
- Avoid jargon overload and over-explaining.
- Use one strong thesis and one supporting line at most.
- Target feeling: quiet authority.

### Visitor Decision

"Do I understand what Vipul does, and is it worth continuing?"

### One Takeaway

Vipul designs systems that turn messy signals into clearer decisions and measurable outcomes.

### Current Diagnosis

The existing hero is visually strong, but the copy still has three layers doing similar work:

- Category label: intelligent systems / product strategy / operational AI
- Thesis: I build intelligent decision systems.
- Support: Products that turn messy signals into clearer decisions, faster action, and measurable business outcomes.
- Extra context: I work at the intersection of AI, behavioral intelligence, operational workflows, and product systems.
- Three label cards: systems thinking, behavioral intelligence, measurable outcomes.

This is not wrong, but it asks the visitor to process the same idea several times. For a clarity-first site, the hero should collapse those layers.

### Hero Copy Standard

The hero should use:

- One category cue.
- One thesis.
- One support line.
- No explanatory sub-grid.
- No repeated concept labels unless they add navigation or proof.

### Production Pass V3

Category cue:

Operational AI / Decision Systems

Thesis:

I build systems that turn signals into better decisions.

Support:

Across AI, data, and workflows, I make intelligence operational: clear choices, faster action, measurable feedback.

Primary actions:

- See systems
- Read thinking

Micro-structure:

Signal → Decision → Action → Outcome → Feedback

Supporting asset decision:

Retire the large hero diagram. It was visually stronger than the original pale version, but it did not earn its first-viewport real estate. The micro-structure now carries the systems concept directly and previews the homepage spine without requiring an explanatory label.

### Why This Is Better

- Shorter and more direct than "I build systems that turn messy signals into clearer decisions."
- Makes "better decisions" the emphasized outcome and uses accent color only for decision meaning and primary interaction.
- Gives the hero a real action path without adding explanatory clutter.
- Replaces the washed-out diagram pattern with semantic text structure, stronger contrast, and tighter first-viewport composition.
- Preserves the positioning around AI, data, workflow, speed, learning, and measurable outcomes.
- Makes the hero behave more like a clarity demonstration than a concept explanation.

### Hero Target Score

Corrected baseline: 6.5/10

After Production Pass V3: 8/10

Remaining path to 10/10:

- Decide whether a future hero artifact can add proprietary meaning without stealing attention from the thesis.
- Continue tuning contrast and first-viewport proportions on real laptop and mobile screens.
- Tune the hero after adjacent sections are rewritten so the first transition feels inevitable.
- Re-evaluate the CTA pair once the Systems and Thinking sections have production copy.

### Production Pass V4

Hero thesis:

Systems for better decisions.

Core design move:

Move the spine out of the hero and into a pinned operating-loop assembly immediately after the hero.

Layer mapping:

- Identity → Signal
- Systems → Decision
- Thinking → Action
- Experiments → Outcome
- Philosophy → Feedback

Why this changes the benchmark:

- The hero no longer carries the whole explanation in words.
- The first scroll becomes the website's signature visual move.
- Section rhythm comes from state progression rather than disconnected blocks.
- The spine becomes structural: it highlights, advances, and connects each layer.
- Motion is scroll-driven, so it expresses assembly rather than decoration.

After Production Pass V4: 8.6/10

Remaining path to 10/10:

- Tune the exact layer visuals after live review so each transition feels inevitable.
- Strengthen the Systems → Decision transition with more specific proof behavior.
- Add a reduced-motion fallback that preserves state clarity without pinned animation.
- Re-evaluate mobile pacing once the assembly is tested on an actual phone.
