# Design Principles Guardrail for Local Agents

## Purpose

This document keeps local agents aligned on the design principles behind the personal website.

The goal is not to make the site decorative. The goal is to make it clear, memorable, low-friction, visually disciplined, and emotionally intelligent.

Use this before changing layout, spacing, typography, color, motion, texture, or section composition.

---

## Core Design Standard

The website should feel like:

- a thinking environment, not a normal portfolio
- a system unfolding, not stacked homepage sections
- premium paper plus quiet intelligence, not startup gloss
- content-shaped space, not containers waiting for content
- restrained, confident, spatially deliberate

Avoid:

- generic dashboard/card aesthetics
- excessive separators
- overuse of small-caps metadata
- decorative texture that hurts readability
- repeating the same layout formula section after section
- overusing the Signal → Decision → Action → Outcome → Feedback spine visibly

# 1. Figma’s 13 Core Graphic Design Principles

Figma’s resource library frames 13 core principles of graphic design:

1. Alignment
2. Contrast
3. Balance
4. Hierarchy
5. Color
6. White space
7. Proportion
8. Repetition
9. Rhythm
10. Movement
11. Emphasis
12. Proximity
13. Unity

## 1. Alignment

Alignment creates calm, order, and authority.

Rules:
- Use strong invisible grids.
- Align headings, section labels, visual artifacts, and CTAs deliberately.
- Do not center everything by default.
- Misalignment is allowed only when it creates intentional tension.

Section guidance:
- Systems can use strict alignment.
- Experiments can selectively break alignment.
- Philosophy can use centered alignment for resolution.

Avoid:
- random offsets
- multiple accidental left edges
- visual elements that appear placed rather than composed

---

## 2. Contrast

Contrast creates clarity.

Contrast can come from:
- scale
- spacing
- density
- opacity
- texture
- color
- surface depth
- typography weight

Rules:
- Primary text must be highly readable.
- Background texture must never reduce legibility.
- Muted blue should mark intelligence, decisions, links, or continuity.
- Avoid beige-on-beige mush.

Good use:
- large hero claim against quiet background
- dense Systems section followed by quiet Thinking section
- accent blue reserved for key decision points

Avoid:
- low-contrast metadata everywhere
- decorative color fields behind important text
- embossed/engraved text that cannot be read

---

## 3. Balance

Balance visual weight, not symmetry.

Rules:
- Use asymmetrical balance intentionally.
- A large text block can be balanced by a smaller but denser artifact.
- Empty space can act as counterweight.
- Do not use “text left, graphic right” for every section.

Good use:
- hero may use text dominance with a secondary artifact
- Systems can become full-width architecture
- Philosophy can become sparse and centered

Avoid:
- every section having the same two-column rhythm
- every visual area having similar weight

---

## 4. Hierarchy

Hierarchy tells the visitor where to look first, second, and third.

Rules:
- One dominant idea per section.
- Do not give every label, card, and visual cue equal importance.
- Hero statement should dominate the opening.
- Secondary content should support, not compete.

Hierarchy order:
1. Hero / section thesis
2. Section focal idea
3. Supporting detail
4. Metadata / cues

Avoid:
- too many cards with similar weight
- labels competing with the actual message
- small-caps metadata everywhere

---

## 5. Color

Color should behave like a system, not decoration.

Current palette direction:
- Paper base: warm off-white / parchment
- Primary text: near-charcoal
- Secondary text: warm gray-brown
- Accent: muted blue `#3D5A80` / stronger variant `#2F4D72`
- Surfaces: warm paper layers

Rules:
- Accent blue is for intelligence, direction, decision, or interaction.
- Avoid colored background blobs behind text.
- Use color sparingly enough that it means something.

Avoid:
- blue everywhere
- atmospheric color spheres that reduce readability
- color used only because a section feels empty

---

## 6. White Space / Negative Space

Whitespace is structure.

Rules:
- Use whitespace before separators.
- Related elements should be grouped by proximity.
- Conceptual transitions should breathe.
- Do not fill space just because space exists.

Good use:
- open hero
- sparse philosophy
- slow thinking section

Avoid:
- packing every section with copy
- adding cards because the layout has room
- using separators when spacing can solve grouping

---

## 7. Proportion

Proportion controls emotional weight.

Rules:
- Hero should feel larger than everything else.
- Systems can be dense but must not overpower the page.
- Supporting copy must be visibly subordinate to claims.
- Visual artifacts should scale according to role.

Avoid:
- similar card sizes everywhere
- similar typography scale across sections
- medium-density sameness

---

## 8. Repetition

Repetition creates unity. Over-repetition creates boredom.

Rules:
- Repeat the mental model, not the exact component.
- Section markers may be consistent.
- Material logic should repeat.
- Layout pattern should not repeat too much.

Good repetition:
- consistent section markers
- recurring paper material
- recurring muted-blue decision cues

Avoid:
- every section using skeleton bars
- every section using cards
- every section using left-text/right-graphic

---

## 9. Rhythm

Rhythm is the pacing of attention.

Target homepage rhythm:
- Hero: open, decisive
- Identity: spacious, reflective
- Systems: dense, architectural
- Thinking: slow, engraved/editorial
- Experiments: fragmented, dynamic
- Philosophy: sparse, resolved
- Closing: warm, invitational

Rules:
- Avoid medium-density across all sections.
- Alternate quiet and dense sections.
- Use rhythm to keep the visitor scrolling.

---

## 10. Movement

Movement is how the eye travels, not only animation.

Rules:
- Guide the eye through scale, spacing, alignment, and directional cues.
- Use motion only when it supports attention.
- Do not animate for decoration.
- The visual spine may exist as underlying logic, but should not be repeated visibly everywhere.

Avoid:
- too many visible arrows/rails/paths
- motion that distracts from reading
- repeating the full spine phrase too often

---

## 11. Emphasis

Emphasis should be rare.

Rules:
- Each section gets one emphasized idea.
- Accent color should emphasize only critical elements.
- Avoid emphasizing metadata.
- Avoid multiple focal points inside the same viewport.

Avoid:
- every card having a badge, number, accent, and label
- too many blue elements

---

## 12. Proximity

Proximity tells the brain what belongs together.

Rules:
- Related elements should be closer.
- Unrelated elements should separate through space.
- Prefer proximity before boxes or separators.
- Cards should exist only when common-region grouping is truly needed.

Agent rule:
Do not add a separator until proximity has failed.

---

## 13. Unity

Unity makes the whole site feel authored.

Rules:
- Maintain a consistent material system.
- Maintain a consistent tone of voice.
- Maintain a limited color palette.
- Maintain conceptual continuity without literal repetition.

Avoid:
- every section looking identical
- every section using unrelated visual styles
- decoration replacing system

# 2. Gestalt Principles for This Website

Gestalt principles explain how people naturally group and interpret visual information. Use them to reduce cognitive load.

## Proximity

Elements close together are perceived as related.

Application:
- Use spacing to group content before using dividers.
- Keep labels close to what they describe.
- Increase distance between conceptual layers.

Agent rule:
Spacing first. Separator second.

---

## Similarity

Elements that look similar are perceived as related.

Application:
- Use similar typography for similar roles.
- Keep section markers visually consistent.
- Avoid using the same visual style for different content types.

Agent rule:
Similarity should clarify relationships, not make everything look the same.

---

## Continuity

The eye follows continuous lines, paths, and implied motion.

Application:
- Use alignment and rhythm to guide scroll.
- Use the visual spine as underlying continuity, not constant visible decoration.
- Let sections hint at the next section.

Agent rule:
Continuity should feel natural, not overdrawn.

---

## Closure

People complete incomplete forms mentally.

Application:
- Do not over-explain diagrams.
- Leave some visual tension.
- Use partial structures to create curiosity.

Agent rule:
Give enough to infer, not so much that the design becomes a manual.

---

## Figure / Ground

Users distinguish foreground from background.

Application:
- Important text must clearly separate from paper texture.
- Background must never compete with content.
- Ambient depth must support legibility.

Agent rule:
If the foreground is not instantly readable, the design has failed.

---

## Common Region

Elements inside the same visual region are perceived as grouped.

Application:
- Use panels only when content truly belongs together.
- Avoid wrapping everything in cards.
- Use common region sparingly for complex systems/proof.

Agent rule:
Boxes are powerful but expensive. Do not overuse them.

---

## Common Fate

Elements moving or pointing in the same direction feel related.

Application:
- Use subtle directional logic in diagrams and scroll flow.
- Avoid too many competing directional cues.
- Motion should reinforce conceptual movement.

Agent rule:
Movement should create coherence, not visual noise.

---

## Symmetry and Order

Humans prefer ordered structures when interpreting complexity.

Application:
- Systems section can use order to create credibility.
- Experiments can break order to suggest exploration.
- Philosophy can return to order for closure.

Agent rule:
Break order only after establishing it.

---

# 3. Nielsen Norman Group UX Principles To Apply

NN/g emphasizes that visual hierarchy guides attention and can be created through color, contrast, scale, grouping, and spacing.

## Visual Hierarchy

Rules:
- The user should know what matters first within 2 seconds.
- Every viewport needs one primary focal point.
- Use scale before decoration.
- Use spacing before separators.
- Use grouping before labels.

## Grouping

Rules:
- Use proximity and common region carefully.
- Do not make every item a card.
- Related content should look related without extra explanation.

## Cognitive Load

Rules:
- Every visible element asks the user to process it.
- Reduce unnecessary labels.
- Avoid visual explanations that repeat what layout already communicates.
- Never use texture, motion, or symbolism at the cost of comprehension.

# 4. Additional Design Principles

## Pattern

Patterns create predictability.

Use:
- consistent section markers
- recurring paper material
- recurring muted blue decision cue

Avoid:
- pattern so strong it becomes monotony

## Variety

Variety sustains attention.

Use:
- different layout grammar per section
- different density per section
- different material behavior per cognitive role

Avoid:
- random visual changes
- novelty without purpose

## Simplicity

Simplicity is resolved priority.

Use:
- fewer words
- fewer containers
- fewer labels
- fewer visual metaphors

Avoid:
- stripping away meaning
- making the site generic

## Accessibility and Readability

Rules:
- Primary text must remain high contrast.
- Small text should not be too faint.
- Avoid text over busy texture.
- Avoid animated or decorative elements that block comprehension.
- Test desktop and mobile separately.

# 5. Material Logic for This Website

Use material behavior to reflect meaning.

## Raised

Meaning:
Operational systems, proof, architecture, build artifacts.

Use for:
- Systems
- case previews
- product architecture
- operational maps

Avoid for:
- philosophy
- deep thinking
- quiet identity moments

## Engraved / Inset

Meaning:
Thinking, belief, reflection, philosophy.

Use for:
- Thinking
- Philosophy
- reflective editorial surfaces

Rules:
- Use inset shadow, contained texture, subtle edge light.
- Do not make text hard to read.
- Do not fake heavy 3D embossing.

## Flat / Open

Meaning:
Identity, self-definition, direct thesis.

Use for:
- Hero
- Identity
- reflective openings

Rules:
- Let whitespace do the work.
- Avoid excessive boxes.

## Fragmented

Meaning:
Experiments, frontier, exploration, unfinished systems.

Use for:
- Experiments
- early ideas
- speculative concepts

Rules:
- Break grid selectively.
- Keep enough order to avoid chaos.

# 6. Rules for the Visual Spine

The underlying conceptual spine is:

Signal → Decision → Action → Outcome → Feedback

It should not be repeated literally everywhere.

## Correct Use

Use it as:
- hidden organizing logic
- section sequence
- diagram behavior
- scroll rhythm
- occasional accent cue

## Incorrect Use

Do not:
- repeat the full phrase in every section
- turn it into a slogan everywhere
- add visible rails/paths in every viewport
- use it as decoration

Agent rule:
The spine should be felt more than read.

# 7. Section-Level Design Strategy

## Hero

Job:
Create authority and curiosity.

Rules:
- One strong thesis.
- One supporting line at most.
- Visual artifact should be secondary.
- No over-explaining.
- No visible overuse of the spine.

Target feeling:
Quiet authority.

## Identity

Job:
Clarify that this is not a resume/portfolio.

Rules:
- Spacious.
- Low text.
- Strong positioning statement.
- Minimal proof.

Target feeling:
A mind entering the room.

## Systems

Job:
Show proof.

Rules:
- Dense but organized.
- Architecture, not gallery.
- Short system names and outcomes.
- Strong relationships, not paragraphs.

Target feeling:
Operational credibility.

## Thinking

Job:
Show reasoning.

Rules:
- Editorial but not text-heavy.
- Essay titles and sharp theses.
- Engraved/reading-plane behavior.
- Let fewer ideas breathe.

Target feeling:
A serious mind at work.

## Experiments

Job:
Show frontier energy.

Rules:
- Fragmented layout.
- Short provocations.
- Uneven rhythm.
- Avoid long explanations.

Target feeling:
Curious, alive, unfinished.

## Philosophy

Job:
Resolve worldview.

Rules:
- Sparse.
- Centered or quietly engraved.
- Few belief statements.
- High negative space.

Target feeling:
Calm conviction.

## Closing

Job:
Invite continuation.

Rules:
- One strong invitation.
- Three paths max.
- Warm, not salesy.

Target feeling:
The next loop is natural.

# 8. Local Agent Checklist

Before making any design change, answer:

1. What is the content job of this section?
2. What is the primary focal point?
3. Can spacing/grouping solve this before adding a separator?
4. Is the text readable against the background?
5. Is the section using the correct material logic?
6. Is the layout different for a reason, or just different?
7. Is the visual spine being used too literally?
8. Can we remove one label, one box, or one decorative element?
9. Does this section increase or reduce cognitive load?
10. Does the page feel more inevitable after this change?

If the answer to #10 is no, do not ship the change.
