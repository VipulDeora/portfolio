## Hero Doctrine

The hero should feel like:
- a calm entry into a thinking environment
- a flat/open thought unit
- quiet authority
- clarity through restraint
- premium paper with disciplined depth

The hero should not feel like:
- a startup landing page
- a normal portfolio banner
- a SaaS product hero
- a blue-branded marketing block
- an over-explained systems diagram

## Non-Negotiables

- One strong thesis.
- One supporting line.
- Visual artifact is secondary.
- Accent blue is used as punctuation: decision cue, active node, link, or one small structural mark.
- Depth comes from value range, edge discipline, paper layering, shadow, density, and figure/ground separation.
- No large accent-color slabs.
- No text over the diagram.
- No repeated visible spine language.
- No explanatory artifact labels unless they are doing real work.

## Version A: Quiet Operating Map

Thesis:

I design systems for better decisions.

Support:

Turning messy signals into clearer action across AI, data, and operational workflows.

Visual:

A raised paper map with stronger charcoal lines, a few muted blue active nodes, and one precise blue decision path. The artifact stays behind and to the right, low enough that it never competes with the headline.

Why it fits:

This is the most aligned version. It keeps the hero calm and editorial while solving depth through shadow, darker surface contrast, and a restrained blue signal.

## Version B: Decision Quality Lead

Thesis:

Decision quality is the product.

Support:

I build AI, data, and workflow systems that turn weak signals into faster, measurable action.

Visual:

A sparse decision-loop trace embedded into paper, with no large panel. Blue appears only at the final decision node.

Why it fits:

This is the boldest intellectual version, but it may be too abstract before the visitor understands what Vipul does.

## Version C: Systems Builder Lead

Thesis:

I build decision systems for operational AI.

Support:

Systems that convert behavioral signals into clearer choices, faster execution, and measurable outcomes.

Visual:

A compact raised system artifact with three neutral stages and one blue active transition.

Why it fits:

This is clear and professional, but it risks sounding closer to a conventional positioning headline.

## Selected Direction

Use a type-led operating-loop hero as the production direction.

Reason:

The right-side artifact consumed too much first-viewport real estate for too little semantic return. The stronger direction is a single editorial hero with one thesis, one supporting line, and a compact operating loop that carries the systems idea without becoming decorative.

## Production Direction: Type-Led Operating Loop

Eyebrow:

Operational AI / Decision Systems

Thesis:

I build systems that turn signals into better decisions.

Support:

Across AI, data, and workflows, I make intelligence operational: clear choices, faster action, measurable feedback.

Micro-structure:

Signal → Decision → Action → Outcome → Feedback

Why it fits:

- It makes the first read faster and more decisive.
- It removes the unlabeled diagram instead of forcing a label onto weak meaning.
- It uses accent blue only as a decision cue and primary interaction cue.
- It creates depth through hierarchy, contrast, paper tone, and spatial restraint rather than a large visual panel.
- It previews the homepage spine while preserving the hero as a calm identity/reflection field.

## Production Direction V4: Threshold + Pinned Assembly

Hero:

Systems for better decisions.

Hero role:

The hero should act as a threshold, not the full explanation. It uses one claim and a quiet signal-to-decision field so the key idea is felt visually before it is described.

Post-hero mechanism:

The spine moves out of the hero and becomes a pinned operating-loop assembly on the next scroll.

Layer mapping:

- Identity → Signal
- Systems → Decision
- Thinking → Action
- Experiments → Outcome
- Philosophy → Feedback

Interaction principle:

Scrolling should not simply move the page. It should advance the system. The top spine highlights the active element while the lower layer gives way to the next layer through scroll-driven motion.

Why this is stronger:

- It reduces hero wording instead of compressing more copy into the first viewport.
- It turns the site into a system unfolding, matching the core artifact direction.
- It makes the spine structural, not decorative.
- It creates rhythm through state change, not stacked section repetition.
- It gives each homepage layer a role in the operating loop.
