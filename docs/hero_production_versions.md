## Hero Doctrine

The hero should feel like:
- a calm entry into a thinking environment
- a flat/open thought unit
- quiet authority
- clarity through restraint
- premium paper with disciplined depth

The hero should not feel like:
- a startup landing page
- a normal portfolio banner
- a SaaS product hero
- a blue-branded marketing block
- an over-explained systems diagram

## Non-Negotiables

- One strong thesis.
- One supporting line.
- Visual artifact is secondary.
- Accent blue is used as punctuation: decision cue, active node, link, or one small structural mark.
- Depth comes from value range, edge discipline, paper layering, shadow, density, and figure/ground separation.
- No large accent-color slabs.
- No text over the diagram.
- No repeated visible spine language.
- No explanatory artifact labels unless they are doing real work.

## Version A: Quiet Operating Map

Thesis:

I design systems for better decisions.

Support:

Turning messy signals into clearer action across AI, data, and operational workflows.

Visual:

A raised paper map with stronger charcoal lines, a few muted blue active nodes, and one precise blue decision path. The artifact stays behind and to the right, low enough that it never competes with the headline.

Why it fits:

This is the most aligned version. It keeps the hero calm and editorial while solving depth through shadow, darker surface contrast, and a restrained blue signal.

## Version B: Decision Quality Lead

Thesis:

Decision quality is the product.

Support:

I build AI, data, and workflow systems that turn weak signals into faster, measurable action.

Visual:

A sparse decision-loop trace embedded into paper, with no large panel. Blue appears only at the final decision node.

Why it fits:

This is the boldest intellectual version, but it may be too abstract before the visitor understands what Vipul does.

## Version C: Systems Builder Lead

Thesis:

I build decision systems for operational AI.

Support:

Systems that convert behavioral signals into clearer choices, faster execution, and measurable outcomes.

Visual:

A compact raised system artifact with three neutral stages and one blue active transition.

Why it fits:

This is clear and professional, but it risks sounding closer to a conventional positioning headline.

## Selected Direction

Use Version A as the production direction.

Reason:

It best matches the artifacts: restrained, operational, clear, human, systems-oriented, and not portfolio-like. It also gives the design room to create contrast without making accent blue the dominant surface.
